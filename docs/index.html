Agent identity & accountability

Each agent has a unique identity, authenticated on every action. Know who owns it, which version is running, and who approved its actions.

Containment & enforcement

The sandbox keeps the agent in.
Warden decides what gets out.

Fort runs each agent in an isolated sandbox built on NVIDIA OpenShell. Network access is restricted to the destinations you declare. Governed tool calls go through Warden and Fort’s relay before reaching tool servers.

Inside the sandboxAgent→Policy checkpointWarden→Approved calls onlyTool server

Warden is the part of Fort that decides and enforces what agents may do. It combines policy evaluation, tool-call enforcement, human approvals and the stop switch.

Policy as code

Rules you can read,
review and diff.

Agents, tools and rules live in a repo and go through review like any other change. You write YAML. Fort validates it and compiles it into the policies Warden enforces, using Cedar for tool-call decisions.

Default deny. A call that matches no rule is denied.

Clear precedence. Deny beats ask, and ask beats allow.

Fail closed. A policy evaluation error denies the call.

What this rule means

The agent can use the ops tool server under its referenced read rules. A restart or rollback in checkout or payments asks for approval. Any matching deny rule still wins, and a call with no matching rule is denied.

Where Cedar fits

YAML is Fort’s authoring format. The compiler validates your configuration, creates the policy representation, and generates a Cedar schema and policies. You do not need to maintain a second set of handwritten Cedar policies.

YAML in your repo→Validate & compile→Cedar evaluation→Warden’s decision

Fort’s policy layer combines the matching outcomes with the precedence above. ask is a Fort decision that starts an approval flow; it is not a standalone Cedar keyword.

Explain the policy you enforce

fort explain agent describes what an agent can and cannot do, generated from the effective policy. It helps reviewers see the boundaries before an agent acts.

Human oversight

Risky actions wait for a person.

An ask decision does not execute the tool call. It creates a pending approval for a named person. The approver reviews the action and completes a fresh sign-in before granting it.

  1. Warden holds the request.The agent’s action has not happened.
  2. A person reviews the exact action.They can approve or deny it. An expired approval provides no permission.
  3. A grant permits the approved retry.A one-time grant applies to that exact call. Warden checks the retry against the active policy.
  4. The decision leaves evidence.The approval and subsequent action are recorded.

A human approval does not override a policy deny or the stop switch.

Audit & governance

Governance you can prove.

Regulators and auditors want to know who allowed an agent to act, and to see proof. Fort answers both by default, from records you hold in your own cloud.

Allowed and denied calls are recorded in a hash-chained ledger. The record connects the agent’s identity, the policy decision and any human approval. Evidence can be verified independently, offline.

fort ledger verifyVerify the evidence independently.

Human oversight. Records you hold.

Fort is designed to support the oversight and record-keeping work security teams do around frameworks such as the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and OWASP, and Japan’s AI Promotion Act, AI Guidelines for Business (METI/MIC) and FSA AI Discussion Paper.

These are governance contexts, not claims of certification or automatic compliance. Ready-made control maps and evidence packs are coming soon.

In your cloud

From deployment to evidence.

Fort is self-hosted. Agents run as OCI workloads in your infrastructure. Policies and evidence stay under your control.

fort up
Set up Fort and its sandbox environment.
fort apply
Apply your reviewed agent and policy declarations.
Warden
Check tool calls and hold risky actions for approval.
fort ledger verify
Verify the audit trail independently.

Model-provider options include Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex, Azure OpenAI and compatible self-hosted endpoints such as vLLM or Ollama. Human workflows use Slack and OIDC.
